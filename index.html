The real problem

Companies are building
Frankensteins.

One team is on ChatGPT, another is on Claude, sales bought Copilot, and someone in operations found a tool nobody else has heard of. Each department has solved its own problem, and none of the pieces are connected or learning from the rest.

That is a collection of experiments standing in for a strategy. The organisations pulling ahead are building one system that runs across the business, learns from every reviewer, and is still theirs when the vendor leaves the room.

Models are converging. The difference is who can make them work together inside your environment.
The pilot is the trap.
A pilot proves the model works, which was never in doubt. A Sprint proves it runs on your workflow, connected to your systems, with your people approving the output, and that is the question a budget turns on.
The right builder matters more than the right model.
What separates outcomes is who can make the models work together in a specific environment, with its data and its rules, and who stays to keep them working.

On every job

Five controls, every build.

Five controls run in every build, from the financial-crime agents, where the approvals and the record are what an examiner will ask for, to the broadcast and music pipelines. The evidence pack that ships with an engagement is assembled from their records.

How each control runs →

Validation before use
Agents and models are measured against your own records or reference material before either is relied on.
Human approval
Model output can reach a decision by one route only: the draft a person edits and approves.
Audit record
Every model call and human decision is written to a hash-chained record, with the cost where the provider reports it.
Release control
Every release passes automated tests before it rolls out, and every delivery is a new numbered, checksummed package.
Data protection
Personal information is tokenised before any model sees it, and routing is restricted in code to approved regions.
