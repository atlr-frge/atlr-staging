The team

Two founders, and the engineers they have worked with for years.

Atelier is the workshop Sean Evans and Michael Chase run for building agentic systems. Sean leads engineering and production, Michael leads strategy and adoption, and artists and engineers build together. We come to each field as outsiders and test against its own records, which is why the financial-crime build was validated against FINTRAC’s published rules rather than against our reading of them.

Michael Chase

Co-founder · Strategy and adoption

Michael was Senior Advisor and Futurist at Deloitte from 2022 to 2025, where he taught AI to partners at Deloitte University and to clients at the Greenhouse, developed the Nvidia digital-twin work for the BMW and Mercedes EV plants, with Nvidia as client and resource partner, and worked with Desjardins on AI in its call centres.

Before Deloitte he founded TRAVAI, an AI travel startup, sold it to Kognitiv, and stayed on as Partner and Chief Strategy Officer, where he built the Kognitiv loyalty engine and developed PC Travel. Earlier, as a Top 100 CMO, he built Cup of Jo for St. Joseph into a franchise with millions of views and counted Google, Thomson Reuters and Nike as clients.

He hosts Materiality, the weekly series on AI with Verma Private Wealth at Canaccord Genuity, writes the Monday Morning Mashup at AI Imagine Labs, and speaks to boards and conferences. AI Imagine Labs is his speaking and publishing company; Atelier is the engineering company he and Sean run. Most recently he gave the keynote at TiECon Canada 2026 in Ottawa on 30 October, Will AI Kill All Humans?, two years after his 2024 keynote in the same room, with CES, SXSW, NRF Big Show in New York, the Digital Signage Expo in Las Vegas and a two-year cross-Canada tour for Canada Post among earlier stages.

Sean Evans

Co-founder · Engineering and production

Sean has spent 28 years building production systems. From 2003, for broadcast series, he was pipeline architect of a production-management platform that ran about 4,000 VFX and CG shots across 26 episodes, with rendering moved to AWS as cloud rendering arrived. From 2015, as co-founder of Occupied VR, he built stereo and 360-degree capture, LiDAR, photogrammetry and volumetric pipelines for Samsung and other clients, with builds shipped on the Oculus store, Viveport and Steam.

He executive produced The Tragically Hip: No Dress Rehearsal, released on Prime Video, and as Animation Director built the AI pipeline that turned thousands of archival photographs into 4K motion. The series had its world premiere at TIFF 2024, took the People’s Choice Documentary Award there, and won seven Canadian Screen Awards, every category it was nominated in. He produced The Holy City, a VR pilgrimage through Jerusalem, an official selection at Cannes XR 2019 and winner of the Advanced Imaging Society’s Lumière award for Best Educational XR, and produced Spots of Light, a volumetric documentary in competition at Venice Immersive 2023 and shown in the SIGGRAPH 2024 VR Theater.

In 2026 he built, for a US network series, the colour, 4K and HDR pipeline for its AI-generated shots, its animated map graphics and the QC of vendor deliverables, and for Warner Music and Daniel Lanois the visual companions, tour campaign and archive agent. Since 2025 he has built with a Toronto compliance platform’s team: the AML agent build, FINTRAC pre-submission validation and a KYC onboarding module. He also did the digital production of Dean Drever’s Eagle at 1 The Esplanade, Toronto, from the artist’s sketches to the model the piece was machined from. He is a member and juror of the Academy of Digital Arts and Sciences, and has spoken at SXSW and SIGGRAPH, where he presented a special session in 2024.

The workshop

One core, many domains.

Each pipeline is built for its domain on the same core, with the points where people step in designed from the start. That core runs across business and creative work: financial crime, network television, music, film, a projection venue and a weekly series on AI.

The enterprise work pays for the engineering and the creative work changes what the engineering can do: Conformer, built for film previsualisation, now holds the canon for a weekly series, and the release control that cuts a tour campaign into numbered, checksummed packages is the same control that gates a compliance release.
